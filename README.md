A 100-word villain monologue for the make.fun bounty
"The Tired Dark Lord: A 100-Word Villain Monologue" — a dark lord
mid-confrontation with the hero, who has ruled for forty years and
is, quietly, running on empty.

## The entry

> You know, dear hero, I built this throne from bones I polished myself. Forty years. Forty years of prophecies, betrayals, one loud volcano. And you walk in with that sword, that speech, that hair. Sit down. Just sit. The crown still pinches. The ravens stopped answering my letters. My curse needs a nap now. I had a plan, you know. A good one. Turn the rivers black, crown the moon, rule forever. But first, I was going to... hmm. What was it? Yes. Win. Go on, then. Strike. Or fetch the blanket, and we'll call it even.

## Why it lands

**The tiredness is felt, never stated.** The word "tired" (or weary,
exhausted) never appears. Instead the fatigue lives in the voice:
short, stalling sentences ("Sit down. Just sit."), a crown that
"pinches", ravens that stopped answering letters, a curse that
"needs a nap now", and a villain who loses the thread of his own
sentence mid-boast ("I was going to... hmm. What was it?"). The
rhythm keeps tripping over itself — forty years of menace, derailed
by small domestic complaints.

**He is a specific villain with a scheme.** This is not a generic
dark lord: he polished his bone throne himself, survived prophecies,
betrayals and "one loud volcano", and still has a real plan — turn
the rivers black, crown the moon, rule forever. The history is
concrete and earned, which is what makes the collapse funny and a
little sad.

**The sting lands in the last line.** He remembers the plan, rallies
("Yes. Win."), invites the killing blow — and then, in the same
breath, trades the whole epic confrontation for a blanket. The hero
came for a duel; the villain offers a draw and a nap. That swap is
the joke, and it only works because the first 90 words took his
menace seriously.
